# Intro to AI Talk — Guidelines for Spanish-Speaking Migrant Parents

**Audience:** Spanish-speaking migrant parents  
**Digital literacy:** Basic — comfortable with WhatsApp and Facebook  
**Format:** Live intro talk (~45 minutes)  
**Goal:** First impression of what AI can do *for them* — make people lean forward, not overwhelm them

---

## The Core Principle

> This is not a curriculum. It is a **first impression.**
> Every topic choice, demo, and word should answer one question in the audience's mind:
> **"Is this for someone like me?"**

For a WhatsApp/Facebook audience, ranking topics by urgency of need is the wrong lens. The right lens is **what lands emotionally in 45 minutes**. Relatable beats important. Visual beats explained. Their words beat your slides.

---

## Talk Structure

```
Wow moment → Personal pain points → Fear honestly addressed → How to start today
```

| Section | Goal | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Open with a wow | Hook — make it undeniable | 8–10 min |
| Make it personal | Show it understands their life | 20–25 min |
| Build trust | Address real fears with respect | 8–10 min |
| Close with next step | One simple action they can take today | 3–5 min |

---

## Topic Ranking for This Context

### Section 1 — Open with a Wow

These two topics create the **live demo moment**. Someone from the audience types something, and magic happens on screen. Do not explain how it works. Just show it.

#### 1. Writing — *your opener*

**Why it works:** Everyone in the room has struggled to write an email in English — to a teacher, a landlord, a doctor's office. This is the pain point they felt last week.

**Live demo idea:**
> Ask a volunteer to describe in Spanish what they want to say to their child's school.
> Type it in, show the polished English email appear in seconds.
> Let the room react before you say anything.

**What to cover:**
- Emails to schools, landlords, employers
- Filling out forms in English
- Texting back a supervisor professionally
- Writing to family back home (WhatsApp messages, voice-to-text help)

**What to skip:** Poetry, social posting, business writing — save for later sessions.

---

#### 2. Nutrition & Healthcare — *pairs with the opener*

**Why it works:** Navigating a prescription, a diagnosis, or a clinic form in a second language is genuinely frightening. Showing AI translate and explain medical language in plain Spanish is immediately emotional.

**Live demo idea:**
> Show a photo of a prescription label or a clinic discharge paper.
> Ask: "What does this mean?" in Spanish. Show the plain-language explanation.

**What to cover:**
- Understanding prescriptions and dosage instructions
- Explaining a diagnosis in plain language
- Finding free or low-cost clinics nearby
- Understanding children's school health requirements
- Food assistance programs (WIC, food banks)

---

### Section 2 — Make It Personal

These topics confirm that AI understands the specific texture of their lives — not generic advice, but their situation.

#### 3. Work & Getting Hired

**Why it works:** Getting a better job is the most common goal in this community. Showing AI polish a resume or prep someone for an interview question in both languages is concrete and exciting.

**What to cover:**
- Writing or improving a resume in English
- Practicing answers to common interview questions
- Understanding a job offer or contract
- Knowing basic labor rights (overtime, breaks, discrimination)
- Translating a job posting and deciding if it's worth applying

---

#### 4. Home Life

**Why it works:** Leases, utility setup, school enrollment, parent-teacher conferences — these are stressful recurring events that require navigating English bureaucracy alone.

**What to cover:**
- Understanding a rental lease before signing
- Writing to a landlord about repairs
- Preparing for a parent-teacher meeting
- Understanding school enrollment paperwork
- Navigating utility or internet sign-up processes

---

#### 5. Personal Finances

**Why it works:** Sending money home, avoiding predatory lenders, and understanding a bank account are universal needs in this audience. The remittances angle is especially resonant.

**What to cover:**
- Comparing remittance services (fees, exchange rates)
- Understanding a bank statement or credit card bill
- Avoiding common scams targeting immigrants
- Basics of filing taxes as a worker
- Simple budgeting help

---

#### 6. Fun & Entertainment — *don't skip this one*

**Why it moves up:** In a live talk, fun is strategic. Showing AI help a child with a story, suggest a recipe, or play a word game removes the "this is for rich tech people" feeling fast. It warms the room and signals that this tool belongs in their home, not just in an office.

**What to cover:**
- Helping kids with homework or a school project
- Generating a bedtime story in Spanish
- Recipe suggestions based on what's in the fridge
- Fun facts or trivia for family game night

**Demo tip:** This is a good moment to invite audience participation — "What's something fun you'd want help with?"

---

### Section 3 — Build Trust

This section must happen. Skipping it leaves people with unanswered fears that will stop them from ever trying.

#### 7. Ethics, Privacy & Hallucinations — *frame this as empowerment*

**Why it must be in the talk:** This audience has been burned by misinformation on WhatsApp. They know what it feels like to share something that turned out to be false. Addressing AI's limitations honestly — before someone asks — signals respect and builds credibility.

**Framing:** Do not present this as a warning. Present it as: *"Here is how a smart person uses this tool."*

**What to cover:**

**Hallucinations**
- AI can be confidently wrong. It sounds certain even when it isn't.
- Never use it for final medical, legal, or immigration decisions without verifying with a professional.
- Good rule: *"If it matters a lot, double-check it."*

**Privacy**
- Do not type full names, ID numbers, Social Security numbers, or immigration status into any AI tool.
- Treat it like a public conversation — share general situations, not personal details.
- Children's information should stay private.

**School and homework ethics**
- AI should help children learn, not do their work for them.
- Teachers can often detect AI-written text. Copying directly can have consequences.
- Best use: explain a concept, check understanding, practice — not write the essay.

**Quick demo:** Show a question where AI gives a wrong answer confidently, then show how to spot and verify it.

---

#### 8. Learning & Personal Growth

**Why it belongs in the trust section:** Connecting AI to the aspiration of learning — English, a GED, a certification — closes the talk on a hopeful, forward-looking note. It also naturally introduces the idea of children using AI for school, which parents care deeply about.

**What to cover:**
- Practicing English conversation with AI (it never gets impatient)
- Studying for a GED or citizenship test
- Learning job-specific vocabulary (construction, healthcare, food service)
- Helping children with reading, math, or science concepts

---

### What to Skip (or Mention in One Line)

| Topic | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Relationships & social skills | One closing line only | Real need, but soft — not a demo topic |
| Caregiver management | One closing line only | Niche, better for a dedicated session |
| Travel | Skip | Low relevance for this audience right now |
| Arts & images | Skip | Creative and fun — but not urgent enough to earn time |
| Programming | Definitely skip | High barrier, low immediate return, risks losing the room |

**Closing line that covers the skipped topics:**
> *"AI can also help with many other things — managing care schedules, planning a trip, even creative projects. Once you are comfortable with the basics, the possibilities are wide open."*

## Demo Tips for a Basic Digital Literacy Audience

- **Use their words.** Ask a volunteer to describe their situation in Spanish. Type their words, not yours.
- **Show, don't explain.** Let the output appear before you comment on it. Silence after a good demo is powerful.
- **Use a big screen and large font.** WhatsApp users are mobile-first — make sure they can read the text from the back of the room.
- **Have a backup.** Wi-Fi at community events is unreliable. Screenshot two or three demos in advance.
- **Normalize mistakes.** When AI gets something slightly wrong during a live demo, use it: *"See — this is exactly why we always read what it says before sending."*
- **End with one action.** Don't give them a list of apps. Pick one: *"Go home tonight and try asking it one question in Spanish. That's it."*

---

## Language Notes

- Conduct demos in both Spanish and English — show that it works in their language.
- Avoid the word "algorithm." Use: *"it learned from millions of texts"* or *"it reads and responds like a very fast reader."*
- Avoid: *"artificial intelligence"* as an opener — start with what it does, name it later.
- Use: *"like a helper that never sleeps and speaks Spanish"* as an early anchor phrase.
